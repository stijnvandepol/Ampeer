**Measured on:** 2026-08-26. Every quotation and every count in this file was
taken that day from the PDFs named below. The filename carries 2026-08-24
because that is the date the lane was opened, and 2026-08-26 is the date to
trust when judging whether this has gone stale. The hedge above the block tables
in section 2 was carried up from the gap list on 2026-08-27; no figure moved.

**Measured against:** the proposal
`codewijzigingsvoorstel-volume-en-tijdsafhankelijke-transporttarieven-voor-kleinverbruikers.pdf`,

not establish. The last part is the important part: the next person builds from
this, and a gap named costs an hour while a plausible sentence costs a rebuild.

## 1. Is the date, and the four levels and five blocks, accurate?

**The date is a request, not a decision, and it carries two written escape
hatches to 2030.**

The proposal asks the ACM "om het voorstel in beginsel per 1 januari 2029 in
werking te laten treden" and then names three dependencies the operators cannot
resolve alone:

- Sector agreements in the marktfaciliteringsforum must be settled by
  **1 November 2027**. If not, the operators ask the ACM to postpone to
  **1 January 2030**.
- If the Minister prioritises energiedelen met vrije leverancierskeuze over this,
  the operators ask for entry into force on "de eerstvolgende 1 januari, 12
  maanden nadat energiedelen is geïmplementeerd", which is an open date.
- If there is no clarity on the Meetregeling and the Regeling gegevensuitwisseling
  Energiewet by **1 December 2026**, again **1 January 2030**.

The ACM's own letter to parliament says only that "Netbeheerders hebben
aangegeven dat de nieuwe tariefstructuur dan vanaf 2029 zal kunnen worden
ingevoerd". The regulator is reporting the operators' claim, not committing to it.

So 2029 is the earliest plausible date and 2030 is a live alternative with two
named triggers, one of which falls due about three months from this reading.
Treat the start year as a parameter, not a constant.

**"Vier prijsniveaus en vijf tijdsblokken" is close and imprecise in a way that
would produce wrong numbers.** The proposal says:

> In de voorgestelde systematiek worden de wegingsfactoren vertaald naar:
> vijf tijdsblokken gedurende een dag, maximaal vier tariefhoogten gedurende een
> dag, en twee seizoenen gedurende het jaar.

and then:

> De wegingsfactoren worden vertaald naar in totaal vijf tariefhoogten, waarbij
> er maximaal vier tariefhoogtes gelden gedurende een dag.

The three corrections that matter:

1. There are **five** levels in total across the year, not four. Annex 5, fifth
   member fixes five wegingsfactorcategorieën in the code text: 0,0 / 0,3 / 0,5
   / 0,7 / 1,0. A model with four levels cannot express the year.
2. Four is a **maximum per day**, and it is never reached in winter. Measured by
   me from the annex 5, sixth member table: summer days use four distinct levels
   and winter days use **three**. A model that assumes four every day will
   allocate volume to a level that does not exist in that season.
3. Five blocks per day holds, but only if you accept that one winter block
   **wraps midnight**. See the next section.

Everything else in the `CLAUDE.md` sentence stands: it is a network tariff, it is
time dependent, and it arrives on top of the end of netting rather than instead
of it.

## 2. The blocks, on an hour axis whose clock is inferred

Source: annex 5, sixth member of the code text, a 12 by 24 table of month
against hour. I extracted it from the PDF with coordinates rather than with a
text dump, because the naive dump misaligned the neighbouring table (see the
warning in section 4). The twelve rows collapse to exactly two distinct
patterns, which is the two seasons.

> **Read the hour columns as inferred, not established.** The proposal heads
> that axis "Uur" and never says which clock it means. I searched all 32 pages
> for tijdzone, wintertijd, zomertijd, UTC, CET, lokale tijd and Nederlandse
> tijd and found none of them, and the TenneT codebesluit does not say either.
> The two tables below are printed in **local Dutch clock time because that is
> my inference**, argued from the summer evening peak landing at 19:00 to 24:00,
> which is a household evening, where continuous winter time would put it at
> 20:00 to 01:00, which is not. It is gap 1 of "What I could not establish" and
> it is the single most consequential unknown here: if the axis is continuous
> winter time instead, every hour below shifts by one for as long as summer time
> runs, which is the whole of the summer table and the October and late March
> part of the winter one, and the shift produces wrong prices rather than an
> error. Whoever wires this reads the tables with that caveat attached or does
> not read them.

**Winter, October through March** (months 10, 11, 12, 1, 2, 3), hours as
inferred local clock time:

| Block | Hours | Weighting factor | Category |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 23:00 - 01:00 | 0,7 | 4 |
| 2 | 01:00 - 07:00 | 0,5 | 3 |
| 3 | 07:00 - 10:00 | 0,7 | 4 |
| 4 | 10:00 - 16:00 | 0,5 | 3 |
| 5 | 16:00 - 23:00 | **1,0** | 5 |

**Summer, April through September** (months 4 through 9), hours as inferred
local clock time, and this is the table the inference is argued from and the one
it would move:

| Block | Hours | Weighting factor | Category |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 00:00 - 03:00 | 0,5 | 3 |
| 2 | 03:00 - 10:00 | 0,3 | 2 |
| 3 | 10:00 - 17:00 | **0,0** | 1 |
| 4 | 17:00 - 19:00 | 0,3 | 2 |
| 5 | 19:00 - 24:00 | 0,7 | 4 |

Measured by me from the table, 2026-08-26: the winter row has **six** contiguous
runs within a calendar day and the summer row has five. Winter reaches the
promised five blocks only because hour 23 and hour 0 both carry 0,7, so
23:00 to 01:00 is one block that crosses midnight. Summer does not wrap: hour 23
is 0,7 and hour 0 is 0,5. This is not a detail. A model that builds blocks by
scanning a day for runs will report six winter blocks and disagree with the
regulator about the count while agreeing about every price.

Hours per category, measured: winter is 12 hours at 0,5, 5 hours at 0,7, 7 hours
at 1,0. Summer is 7 hours at 0,0, 9 hours at 0,3, 3 hours at 0,5, 5 hours at 0,7.

**Per day type: no.** The table has a month axis and an hour axis and nothing
else. There is no weekday, weekend or holiday distinction anywhere in the
proposal. This is worth stating because the neighbouring regime does have one:
the TenneT codebesluit in force since 2025 (Staatscourant 2024, 23593) says
"Voor weekend- en officiële Nederlandse feestdagen stellen de gezamenlijke
systeembeheerders aparte tarieftijdsvensters voor". Small connections get no
such split. Do not copy the high voltage design across.

**Per season: yes, and by calendar month.** Footnote 6: "Met het zomerseizoen
bedoelen de systeembeheerders de periode van april tot en met september. Met het
winterseizoen ... oktober tot en met maart." The boundary is a month boundary,
1 April and 1 October, not an equinox and not the daylight saving switch.

**Per operator: the blocks and factors, no. The euro amounts, yes.** The
wegingsfactoren and the month by hour table are being written into annex 5 of
the Tarievencode, which is national. The proposal is explicit about why: annex 5,
fifth member is added "om de wegingsfactorcategorieën en de wegingsfactoren in de
Tarievencode elektriciteit vast te zetten. Hierdoor hebben aangeslotenen
duidelijkheid over het aantal tarieven, de hoogten van de tarieven en de zekerheid
dat deze twee aspecten alleen met een andere codewijziging veranderd kunnen
worden." The amounts are a different matter, see section 3.

**Revision cadence:** the operators commit to reviewing the blocks and factors
annually and changing them only through a new code change ratified by the ACM.
Energie-Nederland asked in the GEN for a minimum of twelve months between a
decision and its entry into force; the proposal does not grant it. So the table
above is not a constant of nature. It should be stored as dated data with a
validity window, not as literals in code.

## 3. Levels: multipliers, and the amounts do not exist yet

**They are published as dimensionless multipliers.** Annex 5, fifth member is
literally a two column table, category against factor, 0,0 / 0,3 / 0,5 / 0,7 /
1,0. Nowhere in the proposal is there a euro figure for a tariff.

The amount is derived per network operator. Article 3.11b, second member:

> Het kWhgewogen tarief voor aangeslotenen met een kleine aansluiting wordt
> berekend door 67% van de totale toegerekende transportafhankelijke kosten te
> delen door het volgens artikel 3.11c bepaalde kWhgewogen volume.

So each DSB divides its own allowed transport dependent costs by its own total
weighted volume, giving a euro per weighted kWh, and each block's price is that
figure times the block's factor. Since operators already charge different
capacity tariffs today, the euro per weighted kWh will differ between Liander,
Enexis and Stedin. The *structure* is national, the *level* is regional.

Within one operator the price does not vary by connection size. From the GEN
minutes, and this is the operators answering a direct question:

> ESNL vraagt of dat betekent dat het kWh-tarief voor alle kleinverbruikers
> hetzelfde is. Liander antwoordt dat dat inderdaad zo is.

**The ACM says the amounts are not available.** Kamerbrief of 10 June 2026:

> Het codewijzigingsvoorstel bevat nu alleen een berekeningswijze voor de
> tarieven. Bij het ontwerpbesluit zal de ACM meer inzicht geven in de
> daadwerkelijke tariefhoogtes. Netbeheerders moeten hier nog informatie over
> aanleveren bij de ACM.

That is the regulator saying, six weeks before this reading, that nobody has the
numbers. Any euro per kWh figure circulating today is somebody's arithmetic.

**My own arithmetic, so the order of magnitude is on the record.** Stedin's 2026
sheet for a 3x25A connection: vastrecht transport 18,00 euro per year and
capaciteitstarief 291,76 euro per year, both excluding VAT. The capaciteitstarief
is the transportafhankelijk tarief, so it is the pot the 67/33 split divides.
67 percent of it is 195,48 euro. If that same amount were recovered from that
same household through weighted kWh, the euro per weighted kWh comes out as:

| Annual offtake | E1A with solar | E1A without solar |
|---|---|---|
| 2000 kWh | 0,150 | 0,180 |
| 2600 kWh | 0,115 | 0,139 |
| 3500 kWh | 0,086 | 0,103 |
| 5000 kWh | 0,060 | 0,072 |

That spread, a factor of three across a plausible range of household sizes, is
the finding. The real denominator is the operator's entire small connection
weighted volume, not one household's, so none of these cells is a forecast. What
they establish is that the price level cannot be pinned from anything published
and must enter the model as a parameter with a band, exactly like
`SUPPLY_PRICE` and `FEED_IN_COST` in `ampeer_advice/tariffs.py` already do.

For calibration when a real figure eventually appears: HIER, a secondary source,
projects roughly 18 cent per kWh average against a 2030 network bill of 772 euro
including VAT, up from 477 euro in 2026. My table above sits at today's cost
level, theirs at a projected one, which explains most of the gap. Neither is a
tariff.

## 4. Offtake only. This is the answer that matters most, and it is unambiguous

The proposal says it in the toelichting, in one sentence, without hedging:

> Hierbij kijkt dit voorstel enkel naar de afgenomen elektriciteit, aangezien
> aangeslotenen uitsluitend tarieven betalen voor afname en niet voor invoeding
> op het systeem. Indien in de toekomst een invoedingstarief wordt ingevoerd,
> moet opnieuw gekeken worden naar de tarieven voor afname voor aangeslotenen
> met een kleine aansluiting.

The code text agrees at every point. Article 3.11b, first member: "De
tariefdragers ... zijn **voor afname**". Article 3.11c, second member: "het totale,
vastgestelde, **afgenomen** kWh volume". There is no feed-in term anywhere.

So for a household with solar panels, the 2029 tariff prices only what it takes
from the grid. Exported kWh are neither charged nor rewarded by the network
operator. The economic consequence is that the 2029 tariff does **not** add to
the 2027 shock on the export side. It changes the value of *self consumption*,
because avoiding an offtaken kWh now avoids a network charge that varies by a
factor of infinity between summer midday and winter evening.

**A feed-in tariff is being prepared, separately, and not for households.** The
ACM published on 30 April 2026 that it is proceeding with an invoedingstarief
"voor **grote** invoeders van elektriciteit", to be introduced stepwise "op zijn
vroegst vanaf 1 januari 2032". Solar parks, wind parks and power stations. Not
small connections. Two things follow: households are out of scope for the
foreseeable term, and the conditional sentence quoted above means that if this
ever does reach small connections, the offtake tariffs are reopened too.

**And now the finding that is actually about this product.** The corrected annex
5, seventh member, from the nazending of 11 May 2026, splits every profile
category by **afnametype**: AZI is *afname zonder invoeding* and AMI is *afname
met invoeding*. AMI is the household with solar panels. This table exists to
allocate volume for connections without quarter-hour readings, but what it
records is the measured shape of a solar household's offtake across the five
categories, from 2,5 years of settled national metering data. That is a published
national validation target for exactly the profile this project synthesises.

| Profile | Type | cat 1 (0,0) | cat 2 (0,3) | cat 3 (0,5) | cat 4 (0,7) | cat 5 (1,0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1A | AMI | 4% | 12% | 29% | 26% | 29% |
| E1A | AZI | 15% | 16% | 26% | 22% | 21% |
| E1B | AMI | 5% | 12% | 29% | 25% | 29% |
| E1B | AZI | 14% | 15% | 27% | 22% | 22% |
| E1C | AMI | 4% | 12% | 29% | 26% | 29% |
| E1C | AZI | 14% | 15% | 27% | 22% | 22% |
| E2A | AMI | 4% | 13% | 33% | 26% | 24% |
| E2A | AZI | 18% | 16% | 29% | 20% | 17% |
| E2B | AMI | 4% | 13% | 33% | 26% | 24% |
| E2B | AZI | 18% | 16% | 29% | 20% | 17% |
| E4A | (none) | 0% | 13% | 37% | 26% | 24% |

Every row sums to exactly 100 percent, which I checked, and which is one reason
to trust this extraction over the first one.

Weighted average factor per offtaken kWh, computed by me from that table on
2026-08-26: **E1A with solar 0,653 and E1A without solar 0,542**. The solar
household's average offtaken kWh is weighted **20,5 percent higher**.

The mechanism is plain once seen. Category 1, the free block, is summer 10:00 to
17:00. That is precisely when a household with panels is exporting rather than
taking off, so it cannot use the free window: 4 percent of its annual offtake
falls there against 15 percent for a household without panels. Meanwhile 29
percent of its offtake lands in the 1,0 winter evening block against 21 percent.

**This is a real and counterintuitive product finding and it should be stated
carefully.** It does not mean solar households are penalised by design; it means
the tariff's cheap window overlaps almost perfectly with the hours a solar
household already had no reason to buy. The upside a household is told about in
2027, "shift your use to the middle of the day", is worth less on the network
component than it is on the supply component, because the network component in
that window is already zero. It is the *winter evening* where the 2029 tariff
gives a solar household something new to act on, and that is a battery and heat
pump scheduling question, not a laundry timing question.

I want to be honest about how far this carries. The percentages describe an
average AMI connection nationally, over April 2023 to December 2025, which is a
period with less battery uptake than 2029 will have. They are the right thing to
validate a synthetic profile against and the wrong thing to quote to a household
as its own answer.

**A warning for whoever reads the PDF next.** `pdftotext -layout` misaligns both
annex 5 tables: it detaches the label column from the value rows and, in the
1 May version, silently pairs each profile with the wrong afnametype and
produced rows summing to 101 percent. I extracted with word coordinates instead
(PyMuPDF `get_text("words")`, grouping by y). Do not trust a flat text dump of
these two tables. Also note that the 1 May table is superseded: it made no
AZI/AMI distinction at all and its E1A row was 12/15/27/23/23, which is close to
the corrected AZI row and nothing like the corrected AMI row. Anyone who read
only the main proposal has the non-solar shape.

## 5. The capacity component, and how it interacts with the blocks

**It exists, it is a third of the pot, and it does not interact with time at
all.** Article 3.11b, first member:

> a. kWhgewogen, ter dekking van 67% van de kosten;
> b. rekencapaciteit (kW) gebaseerd op de doorlaatwaarde van de aansluiting, ter
> dekking van 33% van de kosten.

The capacity component is keyed on the **doorlaatwaarde**, the fuse rating of the
connection, in one of seven customer categories from annex 6. It is not a measured
peak. There is no monthly maximum, no kWmax, nothing the household's behaviour
moves. A household cannot lower it by shifting load; only by requesting a smaller
connection. It is a flat annual amount and it is orthogonal to the five blocks.

This is a deliberate difference from the large connection and TenneT regimes,
where the time weighting is applied to a measured peak (`kWmaxgewogen`). For small
connections the weighting is applied to energy (`kWhgewogen`). Do not carry
peak-based intuition across.

**The 67/33 split is of the transport dependent tariff only, not of the network
bill.** The proposal: "Er zijn geen wijzigingen beoogd in de tariefstructuur van
de overige tarieven, zoals het transportonafhankelijke tarief (vastrechttarief),
het eenmalige of periodieke aansluittarief en het meettarief." Popular coverage
that says "a third of network costs stays fixed" understates the fixed share,
because the vastrecht, the periodieke aansluitvergoeding and the meettarief sit
outside the split entirely. On Stedin's 2026 sheet for a 3x25A connection those
three are 18,00 + 46,15 + 38,11 = 102,26 euro per year against a
capaciteitstarief of 291,76, all excluding VAT. So the genuinely time dependent
part is 67 percent of 291,76, which is 195,48 euro, or roughly **50 percent** of
that household's 394 euro network bill, not 67.

The operators are explicit that 67/33 was a judgement, not a calculation: "volgt
uit een afweging van bovenstaande argumenten en niet uit een exacte berekening",
and in the GEN minutes Liander declines to call it a best guess and settles on
"een overweging van argumenten". The proposal also says the ratio may be revisited
at the same time as the factors. So it too is data with a date, not a constant.

## 6. What is decided and what is not

**Decided: nothing.** As of 2026-08-26 there is no ontwerpbesluit and no
codebesluit for small connections. The ACM page still reads "De ACM zal het
voorstel van Netbeheer Nederland de komende maanden beoordelen." Everything in
sections 1 to 5 is a **proposal by the network operators**, published by the ACM
for information.

**The state of play, from the Kamerbrief of 10 June 2026:**

- The proposal was filed 1 May 2026 and taken into treatment.
- The ACM expects an **ontwerpbesluit** "aan het eind van de zomer". That is now.
  It had not appeared at the time of this reading.
- The ontwerpbesluit opens a **six week consultation** for all stakeholders.
- The ACM expects a **definitief besluit before the end of 2026**.
- The ACM has explicitly reserved the right to move: it acknowledges the concerns
  raised in parliament, singles out households that installed a heat pump, says
  "De ACM begrijpt dat het belangrijk is dat er handelingsperspectief is om het
  hoogste tarief in de avond zoveel mogelijk te mijden, en betrekt dit bij de
  behandeling van het voorstel", and says it will weigh an EZK study on the
  financial effects per household type. The 16:00 to 23:00 winter block at 1,0 is
  the single most likely thing to move.
- A technical briefing to parliament was offered for September 2026.

**Already decided, and only for other connection types:** time dependent tariffs
on TenneT's high voltage grid have been in force since 1 January 2025
(Staatscourant 2024, 23593). A parallel proposal for large connections on
distribution grids was filed 13 March 2026 and is also pending.

**Practical consequence.** Do not hardcode any of this. The most likely near term
change is the ontwerpbesluit landing within weeks of this file being written,
which is the first document that will carry euro amounts.

## What the simulation core would need

I read `ampeer_sim/timebase.py` and `ampeer_sim/economics/tariffs.py` for this.

### What fits the existing shape, and fits well

**The quarter-hour grid is already the right resolution, and it is the resolution
the regulator settles on.** Article 3.11c, first member: the weighted volume is
found "door de op de aansluiting vastgestelde **kwartierwaarden** te
vermenigvuldigen met de wegingsfactor ... en van de resulterende reeks de som te
nemen". That is an elementwise product of a quarter series with a factor series,
then a sum. `EnergyFlows` already carries exactly that series. Nothing about the
grid needs to change.

**`YearGrid.local_hour` is the hour axis the table needs**, assuming the table is
in local clock time, which is the open question below. It is already derived
correctly for daylight saving, and that derivation is not cosmetic here: if the
tariff really is settled on local clock hours, then the March Sunday genuinely has
no 02:00 and the October Sunday genuinely has two, and the existing
`_local_minutes` reproduces both without special casing.

**`YearGrid.window_mask` already has the branch the winter block needs.** Its
docstring records that the wrapping branch, for a window like (23, 7) whose start
is later than its end, exists but that "the windows in use all read forwards" and
that nothing exercised it on 2026-08-23. Winter block 1 is 23:00 to 01:00. That is
the first real caller for that branch, and it is a caller where the wrong answer,
selecting nothing, would silently drop two hours a day of the second most
expensive winter block for six months of the year. Whoever wires this should note
that the branch was written for this and test it here.

**The 33 percent capacity component fits `TariffSet.standing_charge_year`
exactly.** It is a flat annual euro amount keyed on connection size, which is what
that field already is. So does the vastrecht and the meettarief. No new field.

**Money stays Decimal and energy stays float, with no strain.** The weighted
volume is energy times a dimensionless factor, so it is float all the way; the
euro per weighted kWh is the only money, and it multiplies a scalar sum. The
boundary rule in `CLAUDE.md`, that kWh become euro at exactly one place, is not
threatened.

**The factor series is not a price, so building it outside `tariffs.py` does not
break the rule that no price stands beside an energy series.** It is a calendar
derived multiplier. It belongs next to the grid, or in a small module that takes a
`YearGrid` and returns a factor array. Worth saying out loud, because at a glance
it looks like the kind of thing the rule forbids.

**Battery grid charging is already separable and already billed correctly.**
`EnergyFlows.total_import` folds `grid_charge` into offtake, with the docstring
"Anything that bills the meter should use `total_import`". Under this tariff that
is exactly right: a battery charging from the grid in the winter 16:00 to 23:00
block pays 1,0, and charging at 03:00 in summer pays 0,3. The model can already
express the thing that makes 2029 arbitrage different from 2027 arbitrage.

### What does not fit, and needs a decision rather than an edit

**`YearGrid` has no month.** It exposes `local_hour`, `weekday`, `day_index`,
`quarters`, `hours`, `is_leap`. There is no month axis and no season. The one
place in the package that needs months, `calibration.monthly_share`, walks
`calendar.monthrange` boundaries itself over grid days. So the tariff table's
month axis has no home yet. Adding a `month` property is small; deciding *which
month* is the question in the next paragraph.

**The two axes this table needs currently live in two different time bases, and
the package already does this inconsistently.** `calibration.monthly_share` cuts
months on **grid days**, which are continuous winter time. `calibration.hourly_share`
buckets on **`local_hour`**. Both are defensible for calibration. For a tariff they
must agree, because the season boundary is 1 April 00:00 and the hour boundaries
are clock hours. Whichever is chosen, the disagreement is one hour, twice a year,
at the March and October switches and at the 1 April and 1 October boundaries. It
is small and it is the kind of thing that should be pinned by a test with the
reason written beside it rather than left to whichever property was reached for.

**`annual_cost` has no place to put this, and the obvious place is the wrong
one.** Today the function reduces flows to two scalars and then branches three
ways on `dynamic` / `net_metering` / neither, each branch deciding how offtake is
priced. The network tariff is **not a fourth branch**. It is orthogonal: it applies
on top of net metering, on top of a fixed contract and on top of a dynamic
contract alike, because it is levied by the network operator and the branches are
about the supply contract. Adding it as a branch would make it mutually exclusive
with things it is not mutually exclusive with, and given decision 11 in
`docs/decisions.md`, this project has already been bitten once by a branch
structure that silently dropped a term worth 870 euro.

The shape that fits is an added term:

    network_cost = eur_per_weighted_kwh * sum(total_import * factor_series)

computed once, added to `total` alongside `standing_charge_year`, and independent
of which supply branch ran. `TariffSet` would need the price and the factor
series, or a small `NetworkTariff` value object beside it. That is a design
decision with a real trade-off and I have not taken it; it belongs to whoever
builds the module, and it belongs in `docs/decisions.md` when they do.

**Do not build the factor series with `hourly_to_quarters`.** It interpolates,
treating each hourly value as a midpoint average, and it would invent factors like
0,42 between a 0,3 block and a 0,5 block. The factors are a step function fixed in
a legal code text. They need `np.repeat` or a mask, never interpolation. This is
the single easiest way to get a plausible wrong number here, which is the exact
failure mode `CLAUDE.md` says this package exists to prevent.

**The engine version must move.** Decision 8 pins `ENGINE_VERSION` to the model's
default values. A tariff regime with five factors, two seasons and a price level
is a large addition to that set.

### The one thing to build first, and it is not the tariff

The annex 5, seventh member table is a national, dated, regulator-published
distribution of household offtake across five time categories, split by whether
the household has solar. This project synthesises exactly that quantity from NEDU
profiles and a PVGIS series. Running the existing model's `total_import` through
the block masks and comparing the five shares against the E1A AMI row is a
validation of the *current* engine that costs nothing and needs no 2029 module at
all. It would sit naturally beside `ampeer_sim/calibration.py`, which already
compares modelled against measured shares and already has ceilings that may be
tightened and never widened.

I did not run that comparison. It needs the NEDU profiles, which are not committed
(their licence forbids it, per `docs/decisions.md`), and this lane owns one file
and no code.

## What I could not establish

Named rather than guessed, because the next person builds from this.

1. **Which clock the hour axis is in.** The table is headed "Uur" with columns 0
   to 23 and neither the proposal nor the code text says whether those are local
   Dutch clock hours, continuous winter time, or UTC. I searched all 32 pages for
   tijdzone, wintertijd, zomertijd, UTC, CET, lokale tijd and Nederlandse tijd and
   found none of them. The TenneT codebesluit, which has been in force for over a
   year and might have set a precedent, does not say either, and it indexes its
   hours 1 to 24 rather than 0 to 23, so the two regimes do not even agree on how
   to name an hour. My inference, and it is only an inference: local clock time.
   The summer evening peak sits at 19:00 to 24:00, which is a household evening;
   read as continuous winter time it would be 20:00 to 01:00 local, which is not.
   **This is the single most consequential unknown for wiring the module**, because
   it decides whether `local_hour` is the right lookup or the wrong one, and
   getting it wrong shifts every summer price by an hour for seven months of the
   year without any error appearing. Section 2 carries this caveat above its two
   tables rather than only here, because a caveat four hundred lines below the
   numbers it qualifies is a caveat nobody reads.
2. **How the daylight saving days are settled.** Follows from the above and is not
   addressed anywhere. The March Sunday has 92 quarters of local clock time and the
   October Sunday has 100. Which factor applies to the repeated 02:00 hour in
   October is undefined in the documents I read.
3. **Any euro amount.** Established as absent rather than not found: the ACM says
   in writing that the operators still have to supply the information. The
   ontwerpbesluit is the document to watch and it was expected before this reading.
4. **The E2A, E2B and E4A rows of the fallback table**, in both versions. They are
   printed empty. E4A carries values in the 1 May version and in the nazending, but
   the E2 rows have none in either. This does not block Ampeer, whose households are
   E1A, E1B and E1C, but it means the code text as filed is incomplete.
5. **Whether the 20,5 percent gap between AMI and AZI was intended or noticed.**
   The proposal's own footnote 8, written about the superseded table, argues the
   opposite: "de gesaldeerde variant ... nauwelijks verschil toont met de variant
   met enkel afnamevolumes". The corrected table shows a substantial difference
   between connections with and without feed-in, and the nazending letter presents
   it as an accuracy improvement without commenting on the size or the direction. I
   found no analysis anywhere of what this regime does to solar households
   specifically. If that is genuinely absent from the consultation, it is both a
   gap in the process and, for a neutral adviser, something worth being the one to
   say.
6. **The excel with additional explanation** referenced in the nazending letter as
   an email attachment. It is not on the ACM publication page. It may hold the
   derivation of the corrected percentages.
7. **Whether an ontwerpbesluit has appeared since 2026-08-26.** It was due "aan het
   eind van de zomer". Check the ACM publication page before trusting section 6.
8. **What happens to a household with no smart meter communication.** The mechanism
   is documented well (article 3.11c, second member, using the annex 5 seventh
   member percentages), and the GEN minutes carry an unanswered question from
   Energie-Nederland about whether this invites gaming, since roughly 2 percent of
   connections would receive an averaged profile instead of their own. Not a
   modelling problem for Ampeer today, but it is a real product question if a
   household ever asks whether switching off its meter pays.
